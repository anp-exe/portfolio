a data story

Codédex Staff Pick

2025 ⋆ data analysis ⋆ python · next.js

women in chess 1 in 9

one in nine FIDE rated chess players is a woman. this is a data story about why.

When Netflix released The Queen's Gambit, the chess world expected a revolution. I wanted to know whether any of it showed up in the actual data, so I pulled 130 monthly FIDE snapshots from 2015 to 2026, sampled around 12,000 chess.com profiles, trained a Prophet time series model on pre-show signup data, and ran a Monte Carlo simulation on the gender gap at the elite level.

the show worked. it just turned new fans into online players, not tournament competitors. FIDE could not see the boom because the boom happened somewhere else.

✦ The site gathered thousands of views worldwide, and I was invited to give a talk on it to a London-based chess club. ✦

My entry for the Codédex monthly challenge. After scrapping an AI chess coach idea, the internship hunt sent me to LinkedIn, where the feed was wall to wall AI slop. So I built a detector: a deliberate 50/50 split between transparent rules and a zero-shot Hugging Face model (bart-large-mnli), with an optional shareable score card drawn in Pillow.

don't over-rely on AI to fix your bug. it is an assistive tool, not a programmer. trash in = trash out.

An interactive scrollytelling story built from two public UK datasets: the DfE's Widening Participation statistics and UCAS care-experienced applicant data. More care leavers reach university than ever, yet the gap with everyone else widens the higher you look. 13% vs 46% overall collapses to just 2% vs 13% at the most selective institutions.

the gap does not narrow as you climb toward the most selective universities. it widens to a chasm.

✦ A personal project and a personal story, told through 12 figures on one consistent palette, built from public DfE and UCAS data. ✦
